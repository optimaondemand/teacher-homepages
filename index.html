Course Home Page Builder

Fill in your course details, switch on the sections you want, then paste the generated HTML into a Canvas page and set it as your course home.

1Course details

2Your section & your name

These show up as a row of chips under the course name in the header. Leave any of them blank and that chip disappears — an On-Demand section with no meeting time can skip the time field entirely.

3Meet the teacher

An optional block with your photo, your house, and a link to a fuller page about you. Everything here is optional — leave the photo out and your initials appear in the frame instead.

  1. In Canvas, open Files and upload your photo.
  2. Publish it — click the grey circle beside it so it becomes a green check. Students cannot see an unpublished file.
  3. Click the file to open it, then copy the address out of your browser's address bar and paste it above.

The builder adds the bit Canvas needs on the end of that address to show it as a picture, so you don't have to. Any other web address for an image works here too.

Why a link and not an upload: Canvas strips out photos that are built directly into a pasted page, so a link is the only way one survives.

This is shown as your house, not the course's.

4Announcements

Update these each week. The pinned entry gets the gold highlight. If you'd rather let Canvas feed this automatically, turn the section off and enable Show recent announcements in Course Settings.

5Quick-access tiles

Paste the full Canvas URL for each destination — open the page in Canvas and copy the address bar. The Tech Help tile is pre-filled with the Optima tech support Teams meeting.

6Module cards

One card per module. Link each to that module's intro page (Canvas › Pages › the intro page › copy the URL), or to the module itself in the Modules list.

7Commonplace Corner

8Footer line

The footer already carries the school name, your course name, and the copyright line. Add one more line here if you want — a support email, an office-hours reminder, a class motto.

Putting it in Canvas

  1. Click Copy HTML in the panel to the right.
  2. In Canvas, go to Pages › + Page and title it Course Home (or edit your existing home page).
  3. In the editor toolbar, switch to HTML view — the </> button on the right end.
  4. Select everything already in the box, delete it, then paste. Click Save.
  5. From Pages, open the page's … menu and choose Use as Front Page.
  6. Go to Home › Choose Home Page and pick Pages Front Page.

Every style is written inline because Canvas strips <style> blocks. Editing the page in Canvas's rich-text view can scramble that formatting — come back here to make changes, then re-paste.

1Your Commons

The Optima Commons is your homeroom's gathering place — where your students connect with each other and with the wider OAO virtual campus. This is your Commons, so everything below is yours to change.

2Make it yours

The banner stays Optima navy, so the page always reads as ours. Your accent colours the rule under the banner, the tagline pill, the top edge of every card, and the section underlines. Pick the school colours, or fly your house.

3You, their homeroom teacher

A short introduction to you, with your house. Your house travels with you, so students learn whose colours they are seeing.

  1. In Canvas, open Files and upload your photo.
  2. Publish it — click the grey circle beside it so it becomes a green check. Students cannot see an unpublished file.
  3. Click the file to open it, then copy the address out of your browser's address bar and paste it above.

Why a link and not an upload: Canvas strips out photos that are built directly into a pasted page, so a link is the only way one survives. The same is true of every image on this page.

4Card sections

This is the body of your Commons. Each section is a heading, a line of explanation, and a row of cards — and each card is an icon, a title, a description, and a button that goes somewhere. Three sections come ready to edit: Start Here, House Community, and Help & Support. Rename them, reorder them, delete the ones you don't want, or add your own.

Paste the full address for each button. Open the page in Canvas and copy it out of the address bar.

5Flash poll

A quick question for your homeroom — a temperature check, a vote on a house event, a would-you-rather. Build the poll wherever you like (Microsoft Forms is the easy one), then bring it in here.

This is the reliable one. Canvas cannot break a link.

In Microsoft Forms: Collect responses › Embed › copy. Canvas only allows embedded content from sites it has been told to trust, so check the finished page as a student before you rely on it. If it comes up blank, switch to the button above.

Canvas keeps plain HTML with inline styles and throws the rest away, so a poll that needs JavaScript to count votes cannot work once pasted. What survives is the look of a poll. 6What to do this week

7Meet your principal

This sits at the foot of the page, after the week's work, as the invitation to reach further up. Your principal goes with your grade band, so students see the same face across every homeroom in the band. Leave the photo out and their initials appear instead.

Whatever your principal prefers: a Calendly booking page, a Teams meeting link, a Canvas page, or just their email address. Type a bare email like malvarez@optimaed.com and it becomes a mail link on its own.
