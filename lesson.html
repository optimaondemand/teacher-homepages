1Lesson details

These fill the navy header and the strip of labels beneath it. Anything you leave blank drops out of the strip.

Grade level does not change the layout. It is here so the page records who it was written for.

2Today’s Focus

The gold-topped card that opens every lesson. The hook is a question or a claim in italics; the paragraph underneath says what the student is about to do.

3What students will be able to do

Each line becomes a numbered gold circle under the heading “By the end of this lesson you will be able to…”. Write the verb phrase only; the page supplies “I will be able to”. Do not list standard codes here, they are tracked elsewhere.

4What students need

Texts, notebooks, links, devices, lab items. One per line.

5Lesson content

The lesson is built out of Parts. Each Part is one white card with its own gold heading. Inside a Part you add whatever the content needs: your text, a drawn visual, an image, a video, a call-out box, a reveal box, or an interactive activity. Add as many Parts as the lesson takes.

A drawn visual is a comparison table, a timeline, numbered steps, or a single idea worth remembering, drawn into the page rather than uploaded as a picture. An interactive activity is a set of boxes a student thinks about, then clicks open to check. Both are built from what you type here.

6What students do next

Point students at the graded work that follows this lesson. Those items live in Canvas as real assignments, quizzes, or discussions; this section is only the signpost.

Before you publish

    Putting it in Canvas

    1. Open the HTML to paste tab on the right and click Copy.
    2. In Canvas, go to Pages › + Page and title it with the lesson number and title.
    3. In the editor toolbar, switch to HTML view, the </> button on the right end.
    4. Select everything already in the box, delete it, then paste. Click Save.
    5. Add the page to its module in Modules, and publish it.

    Every style is written inline because Canvas strips <style> blocks. Editing the page afterwards in Canvas’s rich-text view can scramble that formatting, so come back here to make changes and re-paste.
